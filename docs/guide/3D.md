# Annotating in 3D

3D mode opens a sequence as a single volume, with the frame index as the depth axis. You still annotate slice by slice, and two more views appear beside the canvas.

3D mode is [experimental](../experimental.md): it works, but it is lightly tested and will probably change.

## Turning it on

1. Click the wrench button at the right of the top toolbar and switch on
   **Experimental features**.
2. Open a sequence in the editor.
3. Click the cube icon in the **View** group of the editor menu.

The cube only appears on sequences. If it is greyed out, its tooltip says why:
the sequence needs at least two frames of the same size and at least one
segmentation label, and the volume must fit in memory. The exact limits are
listed under [requirements](../experimental.md#requirements).

++shift++ + wheel scrolls through the slices.

## The two views

| View | Shows | Use it to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **3D view** | The labels in space, as surfaces or voxels, with the image as planes or as a rendered volume | See the shape of a structure and spot slices where it breaks |
| **Projection** | The volume unrolled along a surface set by two curves, **A** and **B**, drawn on the slice | Follow a structure through every slice at once, and paint on it |

Both update as you paint. In the 3D view, drag to orbit, right-drag to pan and
scroll to zoom; clicking a surface jumps to that slice. In the projection, the
horizontal line marks the current slice: drag it to change slice.

Each view can fill the editor area or open in its own window, for a second
monitor.

## Painting on the projection

The pencil button in the projection header turns it into a canvas for the
raster tools: pen, line, lasso and the erasers. One stroke there can label many
slices at once, and it is a single step in the undo history.

A pixel of the projection stands for a whole segment between curves A and B.
The **depth** slider chooses where along that segment the paint lands, from
A (0) to B (1).
